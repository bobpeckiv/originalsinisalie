The film

The Kingdom Within

A documentary, 2015 · about 77 minutes

The full film is free on YouTube. Open it on YouTube.

Is yoga just exercise? Is it a Hindu practice that Christians should stay away from? Or is there something underneath both traditions that they share? Before I wrote Original Sin Is A Lie, I spent a few years in Austin asking those questions on camera, of pastors, priests, yogis, and scholars. This film is what came out of it.

About the film

Christianity, yoga, and the kingdom within

I filmed The Kingdom Within between 2011 and 2014, right after finishing my religious studies degree at the University of Texas. It features about twenty voices: Baptist and other Protestant pastors, Catholic priests, Hindu and Kriya Yoga teachers, yoga teachers and their students, and a scholar of Indian philosophy. Running through the middle of it is Richie Flores, the "Tejano Yogi," whose story of finding yoga behind bars and coming out a teacher is the heart of the film.

Nobody in it is set up to be the villain. The pastors who are wary of yoga get to say why, in their own words, and the yogis who love Jesus get to say why too. The film just keeps putting the same questions to all of them and letting you listen.

It was released in 2015 and streamed on Gaia for five years. Now it's free for anyone on YouTube.

From film to book

Where it led

The last chapter of the film takes its title from Luke 17:21, "the kingdom of God is within you," and that verse never let go of me. Years of conversations from this film fed straight into Original Sin Is A Lie: the idea that the mystics of every tradition are pointing at the same thing, and that the Jesus of the Gospels was pointing there too.

If the film is the question, the book is my answer. (Start with Luke 17:21.)
