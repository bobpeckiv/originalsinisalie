A guide

Paul

Saul of Tarsus, letters written in the 50s CE

Paul shaped Christianity as much as anyone, maybe more than Jesus himself. Here's what surprises most people: he never met Jesus in person, and scholars agree he didn't write about half of the letters with his name on them. Some of the harshest lines people quote at you come from that second half.

Who he was

The persecutor who converted

Saul was a Pharisee who persecuted the early Jesus followers (Galatians 1:13). On the road to Damascus, a few years after the crucifixion, he was struck by a vision of light and a voice: "I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting" (Acts 9:5). He spent the rest of his life traveling the Roman world, starting house churches and writing to them.

There's real beauty in that. Love so powerful it turns a persecutor around. And after reading the stories of saints and gurus from every tradition, I don't scoff at visions like his.

But the second most influential person in Christianity never met the first. What we have is two thousand years of telephone: a teacher, then decades of stories, then Paul's take on them, then the gospels, and then, centuries later, Augustine reading Paul to say we're all born evil. (More on that.)

What's really his

Seven letters, maybe more

Almost certainly Paul

Romans, 1 Corinthians, 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Philippians, 1 Thessalonians, Philemon

Debated or unlikely

Ephesians, Colossians, 2 Thessalonians, and the "pastoral letters," 1 and 2 Timothy and Titus, which most scholars date after his death. Hebrews doesn't claim to be by him at all.

Scholars sort them by vocabulary, writing style, theology, and the church situation each letter assumes. The pastorals read like a later, more organized church with bishops and rules, not the house churches of the 50s.

Why it matters

"Let a woman learn in silence"

Let a woman learn in quietness with all subjection. But I don't permit a woman to teach, nor to exercise authority over a man, but to be in quietness.

1 Timothy 2:11–12

That line has kept women out of pulpits for nearly two thousand years. It's in one of the letters scholars think Paul probably didn't write. Meanwhile, in Romans, a letter he definitely wrote, Paul sends greetings to Phoebe, a deacon of her church, and to Junia, whom he calls "prominent among the apostles" (Romans 16:1, 16:7). Pick and choose, indeed.

The gems

Paul at his best

I don't want to throw Paul out. He's not a Christ or a Buddha, but he wrote some of the most beautiful lines in the whole Bible:

Love is patient and is kind; love doesn't envy. Love doesn't brag, is not proud... bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, and endures all things.

1 Corinthians 13:4–7

There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free man, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus.

Galatians 3:28

For now we see in a mirror, dimly, but then face to face.

1 Corinthians 13:12

All one. That's the mystic Paul. That's the one worth keeping.
