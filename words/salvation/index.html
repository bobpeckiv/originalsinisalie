Words / Salvation

The word

Salvation

Greek: sōtēria · verb: sōzō

Most of us were taught that being saved means getting your ticket to heaven after you die. But the Greek word the Gospels use for "save" is the same word they use for being healed. When Jesus tells someone "your faith has saved you," he's usually talking to a person who just got well.

One word, two translations

Saved, or made well

The verb is sōzō. It means to rescue, to keep safe, to heal, to make whole. The noun, sōtēria, is safety, deliverance, health. English Bibles split it into two families of words, "save" and "heal," depending on whether the translators thought the moment was spiritual or physical. In Greek it's one word.

Mark 5:34

"Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace, and be cured of your disease."

Luke 7:50

"Your faith has saved you. Go in peace."

Mark 10:52

"Go your way. Your faith has made you well." And the blind man could see.

Mark 5:23

"Please come and lay your hands on her, that she may be made healthy, and live."

Same verb in every one of those. The woman who touched his cloak after twelve years of bleeding, the woman who washed his feet with her tears, blind Bartimaeus, a little girl near death. Salvation in the Gospels looks a lot like a person getting their life back.

It's in the English too

Heal, whole, holy

The old English words heal, whole, hale, and holy all grow from the same root. To be healed was to be made whole, and to be whole was close to being holy. Latin did the same thing: salus meant both health and safety, which is where we get "salvation," and also "salute" and "salutary." Somewhere along the way the church kept the word and lost the medicine.

Eastern Orthodox Christians never fully lost it. They often describe the church as a hospital for the soul rather than a courtroom, and salvation as a healing that unfolds over a whole life.

When salvation happens

Today, not someday

Today, salvation has come to this house... For the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost.

Luke 19:9–10

Jesus says this to Zacchaeus, a tax collector everyone in Jericho despised, right after Zacchaeus decides to give half of what he owns to the poor and pay back anyone he cheated. Not a word about the afterlife. Salvation shows up in a living room, the same afternoon a man stops being greedy.

The Hebrew Bible speaks the same way. At the Red Sea, Moses tells the terrified Israelites, "Stand still, and see the salvation of Yahweh, which he will work for you today" (Exodus 14:13). Yeshuah, salvation, is rescue in this life. Jesus' own name, Yeshua, means "Yah saves."

Every tradition has a word for it

Being made whole

Hindus call it moksha, liberation. Buddhists call it nirvana, the blowing out of the flames of craving. Sufis call it fana, the passing away of the separate self in God. In my book I write it as "salvation / enlightenment / Unity," because I think they're pointing at the same healing: waking up from the idea that you're separate and broken, and finding out you were whole the whole time.

Paul told the Philippians to "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" (Philippians 2:12). Not wait for it. Work it out, here, in this body, in this life.
