A guide

Talking with family

For when the people you love think you're lost

The question I get most in my DMs, after "what should I read," is some version of this: my mom, my mother-in-law, my best friend, my whole hometown thinks I'm going to hell. What do I say? I'll be honest. I wish I had the magic key to that locked door. Nobody gets argued out of their faith. But some things do help.

Love everyone in the story

I love people who left the thing.

The thing that was controlling. Scary to leave. And they left anyway.

 

I love people still in the thing.

Maybe the thing is so well built they can't even see it. I love them anyway.

 

And I love the makers of the thing.

Because they were so afraid, they had to build something for themselves.

 

The antidote for fear is love. It's the only way out.

Your family isn't the enemy. Fear is. Most of the people who worry about your soul are worried because they love you and they're scared, and that's worth honoring even when it hurts.

What actually helps

A few things I've learned

  1. Keep Jesus as common ground.

    You don't have to attack him to disagree with the institution. "I love Jesus too. I just read him differently" opens more doors than any history lesson. Christianity isn't the problem. Egoism, exclusivity, hypocrisy, and abuse are.

  2. Point to Christians, not to me.

    A lot of devout folks only trust Christian sources, so meet them there. The tradition has its own mystics, and they say what I say: St. Francis of Assisi, St. Teresa of Ávila, St. John of the Cross, Meister Eckhart, Thomas Merton, Howard Thurman, Martin Luther King Jr., Richard Rohr.

  3. Ask instead of argue.

    "What does it mean to you to follow Jesus?" goes further than "your verse is mistranslated." People loosen when they feel heard, not when they lose a debate.

  4. Know the one verse.

    Sooner or later it'll be John 14:6. Here's how I read it, in four points, without any contempt.

  5. You're allowed to step back.

    Loving someone doesn't mean letting them call you damned at every dinner. Distance can be a form of love too, for them and for you.

When they say it

"You're going to hell."

I get some version of this most weeks. Here's roughly how those conversations go:

Me

When I began to love God, I began to see God in everything. In every being, even.

Them

Jesus says he is the way to God. The only way.

Me

Plenty of great teachers said they were the way, because they gave up their separate self and became pure vessels of spirit.

Them

You're going to hell, and you're taking others with you.

And my answer: Okay! Have a really good day.

You don't have to win. If hell means a life without love, then responding with love is already the way out. And if I'm wrong, I'll dance down there with Rumi, Mirabai, and Kabir.
