Verses / Luke 17:21

The verse

"The kingdom of God is within you."

Luke 17:21

If I could keep one line from the whole Bible, it might be this one. Hindu swamis love it. Buddhist monks love it. Anyone who has sat still long enough to taste what's underneath the noise knows it's true. The kingdom isn't a place you go after you die. It's the peace already inside you.

The verse

What he said

Being asked by the Pharisees when God's Kingdom would come, he answered them, "God's Kingdom doesn't come with observation; neither will they say, 'Look, here!' or, 'Look, there!' for behold, God's Kingdom is within you."

Luke 17:20–21

Not here, not there, not something you can point at or wait for. Within.

The translation fight

"Within" or "among"?

The King James and many older Bibles say "within you." A lot of modern translations, including the NRSV and the 2011 NIV, changed it to "among you" or "in your midst." That shifts the meaning a lot. "Among you" can sound like Jesus pointing at himself: the kingdom is here because I'm standing here. You'll find plenty of online apologists correcting us Kumbaya-singing mystics about it.

But look at the Greek word, entos. It shows up only one other time in the whole New Testament:

You blind Pharisee, first clean the inside of the cup and of the platter, that its outside may become clean also.

Matthew 23:26

Nobody translates that "clean the among of the cup." It means the inside. When the New Testament wants to say "among," it usually uses a different phrase, en mesō, as in "lambs among wolves" (Luke 10:3). The scholar Ilaria Ramelli has also shown that the ancient Syriac translations, in a language close to the Aramaic Jesus spoke, read it as "inside you." Some scholars still argue for "among," since he was talking to Pharisees. I'm with the mystics, and with the plain meaning of the word.

It's everywhere

Every tradition found the same room

The Gospel of Thomas, buried in Egypt for sixteen centuries, says it even more fully: "The kingdom is inside you and it is outside you" (saying 3). Leo Tolstoy named his great book on nonviolence The Kingdom of God Is Within You, and it changed the life of a young lawyer named Gandhi. A spiritual mentor once told me Christianity missed Jesus' teaching by one letter. It was never Christianity. It was Christ-I-AM-ity.

Underneath the fear, the anxiety, and all the stories we tell about ourselves, there's perfect peace. That's what every spiritual practice is for: finding that room and living from it. I made a whole film about this, and named it after this verse.
