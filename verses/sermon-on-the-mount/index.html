Verses / The Sermon on the Mount

The verses

The Sermon on the Mount

Matthew 5–7 · Luke 6:17–49

If Jesus had a manifesto, this is it: three chapters of Matthew that hold up as well today as they did two thousand years ago. Gandhi kept it close. Tolstoy said it made a Christian army a contradiction. You don't have to be a Christian to see that it's one of the greatest things ever said.

The blessings

Blessed are the meek?

Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the Kingdom of Heaven... Blessed are the gentle, for they shall inherit the earth... Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy. Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God. Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called children of God.

Matthew 5:3–9

This was practically still the Iron Age. Heads were getting cut off left and right. And here's a teacher saying the gentle, the merciful, and the peacemakers are the blessed ones. That's extraordinary.

A small detail worth knowing: Luke has a version too, the "Sermon on the Plain," delivered on level ground instead of a mountain, and his first line is simply "Blessed are you who are poor" (Luke 6:20). Same teaching, different setting, slightly different emphasis. (More on that.)

The hard part

Turn the other cheek

You have heard that it was said, 'An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth.' But I tell you, don't resist him who is evil; but whoever strikes you on your right cheek, turn to him the other also.

Matthew 5:38–39

Love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who mistreat you and persecute you.

Matthew 5:44

"An eye for an eye" comes straight from the old law (Exodus 21:24), and Jesus explicitly sets it aside. Go beyond reciprocal violence. Go beyond punishment. And the way to do it is love.

This hasn't gotten easier to hear. In 2023, Russell Moore, the editor of Christianity Today at the time and no liberal himself, said pastors were telling him that when they quoted "turn the other cheek" from the pulpit, people came up afterward and asked where they got those liberal talking points.

The rest of it

What else is in there

  • Don't perform your goodness. Give in secret, pray in secret (Matthew 6:1–6).
  • The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9–13).
  • Don't store up treasures on earth. You can't serve God and money (Matthew 6:19–24). (More here.)
  • Consider the lilies. Stop worrying (Matthew 6:25–34).
  • Judge not. Get the plank out of your own eye first (Matthew 7:1–5).
  • Ask, seek, knock (Matthew 7:7).
  • The narrow gate (Matthew 7:13–14). (More here.)

When Gandhi's friend Lord Irwin, the British viceroy of India, asked him how to solve the problems of their two countries, Gandhi is said to have opened his Bible to Matthew 5 and said that when their countries got together on this sermon, they'd have solved the problems of the whole world.
