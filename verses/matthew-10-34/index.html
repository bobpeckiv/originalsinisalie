Verses / Matthew 10:34

The verse

"I didn't come to send peace, but a sword."

Matthew 10:34

When I was about eight, I went with a Baptist friend to Christian summer camp and heard this line for the first time. I remember thinking: wait, I was really liking this guy. Love your enemies was right on. Now he's bringing swords? He's either inconsistent, or somebody got the translation wrong. Turns out it was neither. Nobody at Vacation Bible School had explained the difference between literal and symbolic.

What the sword cuts

Don't think that I came to send peace on the earth. I didn't come to send peace, but a sword. For I came to set a man at odds against his father, and a daughter against her mother.

Matthew 10:34–35

He's not talking about armies. He's talking to the twelve, his inner circle, about what following him will cost. The truth he's teaching will divide households. Luke's version of the same saying doesn't even use the word sword. It says "division" (Luke 12:51).

And when one of his own disciples actually pulls a real sword in the garden of Gethsemane, Jesus stops him cold:

Put your sword back into its place, for all those who take the sword will die by the sword.

Matthew 26:52

If the man who said that wanted literal swords, he picked a strange moment to say it.

A sword of truth

The mystics' reading

In Revelation, the sword comes out of Christ's mouth (Revelation 1:16). Hebrews calls the word of God "sharper than any two-edged sword" (Hebrews 4:12). In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna tells Arjuna to cut through his doubt "with the sword of knowledge" (4.42).

Paramahansa Yogananda, in his commentary on the Gospels, The Second Coming of Christ, reads this verse the same way: Jesus didn't come to hand out comfortable worldly peace. He came with the sword of wisdom, to cut the attachments that keep us asleep, even when that means disruption.

So when someone quotes this verse to justify violence, or Christian nationalism, ask them to keep reading. The sword is the truth. It cuts through illusion, not through people.
