Verses / Matthew 6:24

The verse

"You can't serve both God and Mammon."

Matthew 6:24

Jesus said zero words about original sin. He said a great many about money. A striking number of his parables are about wealth and possessions, more than almost any other subject. And yet the birthday of the most anti-greed man in history is now the biggest shopping season of the year.

The verses

What he actually said

No one can serve two masters, for either he will hate the one and love the other; or else he will be devoted to one and despise the other. You can't serve both God and Mammon.

Matthew 6:24

For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.

Luke 12:34

It is easier for a camel to go through a needle's eye, than for a rich man to enter into God's Kingdom.

Mark 10:25

Mammon is an Aramaic word for wealth, money treated like a god. That needle line has had pastors in designer sneakers sweating for centuries. They do an amazing job of reading around it.

A confession

Christmas is Q4

For years my daily bread was digital advertising, and in that world the most important time of year is Q4: October, November, December. Advertisers plan for it obsessively. Why? Christmas, the "Christ mass," the birthday of Jesus of Nazareth. The man who told us not to store up treasures on earth (Matthew 6:19) is the reason for the biggest explosion of consumerism on the calendar. The paradoxes on this planet never end, but that one's a doozy.

Meanwhile, preachers fly private jets and build domes, and people notice. In 2021, Gallup reported that fewer than half of Americans belonged to a church, mosque, or synagogue for the first time in eight decades of polling.

Money isn't evil

The grip is the problem

Money itself is neutral. Save some, spend some, pay people fairly for good work. The problem is the clinging, when the wanting matters more than anything else. That's idolatry in the most literal sense. Jesus knew what the masters of every tradition knew: a fixation on piling up stuff robs other people, and then it robs us.

  • "Be content with what you have; rejoice in the way things are. When you realize there is nothing lacking, the whole world belongs to you." Tao Te Ching
  • Ramakrishna compared us to children playing with dolls, with wealth, honor, and fame, who drop them all and run the moment their Mother walks in the room.
  • The earliest followers of Jesus "had all things in common" and sold their possessions to share with anyone in need (Acts 2:44–45).
