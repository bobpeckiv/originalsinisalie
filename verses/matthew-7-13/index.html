Verses / Matthew 7:13

The verse

"Enter in by the narrow gate"

Matthew 7:13–14

I've heard plenty of fundamentalists quote this one. The usual reading goes: the narrow gate is Christianity, the one way into heaven, and most of humanity is headed through the wide gate to destruction. But Christianity as an institution didn't exist when Jesus said it. He was talking about something else.

The verse

Narrow is the way

Enter in by the narrow gate; for wide is the gate and broad is the way that leads to destruction, and many are those who enter in by it. How narrow is the gate, and restricted is the way that leads to life! Few are those who find it.

Matthew 7:13–14

It comes near the end of the Sermon on the Mount, right after "ask, seek, knock" and the Golden Rule. It's not a membership policy. It's a description of the path: the easy, wide road is the one everyone drifts down, chasing pleasure and running from pain. The way to life takes attention.

His Indian brother

The middle way

The Buddha told a story about a musician monk named Sona who was practicing so hard his feet bled, and was ready to quit. The Buddha asked him about his lute. If the strings are too loose, can you play it? No. Too tight? They snap. When they're neither too loose nor too tight, you get music.

That's the narrow gate. On one side is indulgence, on the other is harsh self-punishment, and both can hook you. The middle path between them is narrow, and it takes some discernment and some self-awareness to walk. It isn't a joyful skip through the meadow, at least at first. But a few do find it, and they've been telling us how for thousands of years.

Not a velvet rope

Narrow, not exclusive

Luke's version says "strive to enter in by the narrow door" (Luke 13:24). Strive, practice, pay attention. It's an invitation to effort, not a list of who's in and who's out. Read it next to John 14:6 and the "I am" sayings, and the narrow gate isn't a religion. It's the way of love, and it's open to anyone who walks it. (More here.)
