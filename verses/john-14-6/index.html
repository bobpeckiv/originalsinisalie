Verses / John 14:6

The verse

"I am the way, the truth, and the life."

John 14:6

This is the number one verse Christians send me. The whole line reads: "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me." Read with its context, it isn't a velvet rope. It's a master telling his closest friends what union with God feels like from the inside.

Four things to know

How I read it

  1. It's in John, the spiritual gospel.

    John is the last of the four gospels, written in Greek around 90 to 110 CE, roughly two generations after Jesus died. Jesus spoke Aramaic, so even the first written versions of his words were already translations. The church fathers knew John was different. Clement of Alexandria, writing around 200 CE, called it the "spiritual gospel." It doesn't open with a manger. It opens like Genesis: "In the beginning was the Word."

  2. Look at who's in the room.

    Jesus taught on three levels. The big crowds got plain teachings like "Blessed are the peacemakers." Smaller groups got parables, stories that carried the message without getting him arrested (they arrested him anyway). And his closest disciples got the mystical, symbolic teaching. The Gospels say this outright:

    "To you has been given the secret of the kingdom of God, but for those outside, everything comes in parables."

    Mark 4:11

    John 14 is the Last Supper. The door is closed, and he's talking to the people who've lived with him for years. This is the inner-circle level.

  3. Listen to the "I am."

    When Moses asks God for a name, the answer is "I AM THAT I AM" (Exodus 3:14). In John, Jesus says "Before Abraham was, I AM" (John 8:58), about a man who lived well over a thousand years before him. That can't be literal. The I AM is the eternal awareness of God, union with all of it. And in Mark, when a man calls him "Good Teacher," Jesus answers, "Why do you call me good? No one is good but God alone" (Mark 10:18). Back in John, he says it even more plainly: "By myself I can do nothing" (John 5:30). The man from Nazareth is nothing without the I AM consciousness behind him and beyond him. He knew the difference between himself and the I AM.

    So the way is the state he attained: unconditional love, union with the One. It isn't Jesus the man, and it isn't a religion called Christianity, which didn't exist yet.

  4. That's how the avatars talk.

    Read the Bhagavad Gita. For whole chapters Krishna says I am this, I am that, I am time itself.

    "There is nothing whatever higher than Me. All this is strung on Me, as pearls on a thread."

    Bhagavad Gita 7.7

    The Buddha told a man to remember him simply as "awakened." The Sufi Mansur al-Hallaj was executed in 922 for saying Ana al-Haqq, "I am the Truth." Sound familiar? When the separate self is gone, this is the language that's left. It isn't a brag, and it isn't a border.

The short version

So is he the way?

Yes. Love is the way. Union is the truth and the life. He walked it all the way to the end, and he's one of the brightest lights we've ever had.

He's wonderful. He got it. My only question is why we stop at him.
