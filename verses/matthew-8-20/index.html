Verses / Matthew 8:20

The verse

"The foxes have holes"

Matthew 8:20 · Luke 9:58

A man walks up to Jesus and says, I'll follow you anywhere. Jesus' answer sounds at first like a complaint about housing. Read it again, though, and it's one of the most nondual moments in the Gospels: a description of a mind that doesn't live anywhere.

The verse

"Wherever you go"

A scribe came, and said to him, "Teacher, I will follow you wherever you go." Jesus said to him, "The foxes have holes, and the birds of the sky have nests, but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head."

Matthew 8:19–20

On the plain level, it's true. Jesus left his home and his trade in Nazareth and spent his ministry on the road, sleeping wherever people took him in. He's telling an eager recruit what he's signing up for. No house, no pillow, no guarantees.

Son of Man

Or simply, the human being

"Son of Man" sounds like a grand title, and the Gospels do use it that way. But in Aramaic, bar enasha can also just mean "a human being," the way the prophet Ezekiel is called "son of man" again and again, or the way the Psalm asks, "What is man, that you think of him? What is the son of man, that you care for him?" (Psalm 8:4).

Hear it that way and the line opens up. Every creature has its place. The fox has its den, the bird has its nest. But a human being who's woken up has no fixed place to rest in the world. Not in a house, not in a reputation, not in a story about who they are.

The nondual reading

A mind that doesn't dwell anywhere

The Diamond Sutra, one of the most important texts in Mahayana Buddhism, says to let the mind arise without dwelling anywhere. Tradition says Huineng, a poor young woodcutter who became the Sixth Patriarch of Zen, woke up the moment he heard that line read aloud.

That's what the Son of Man is describing. The awareness that sees everything isn't located anywhere. It can't be housed. It has nowhere to lay its head because it isn't a thing among things. It's the seeing itself.

Pair it with another favorite: "If thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light" (Matthew 6:22). One undivided eye, and no place it calls home. Two sides of the same nondual coin. (More on the single eye.)

Every tradition

Passersby

  • "Become passersby." Gospel of Thomas, saying 42
  • "Be in this world as if you were a stranger or a traveler." Muhammad, in the Hadith collected by al-Bukhari
  • On the great gate of Fatehpur Sikri in India, the emperor Akbar had carved a saying attributed to Jesus: "The world is a bridge. Pass over it, but build no house upon it."

We are tourists here. It isn't a call to hate the world. It's a call to stop clinging to it, to enjoy the scenery without mistaking the hotel for home.
