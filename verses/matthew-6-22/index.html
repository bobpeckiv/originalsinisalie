Verses / Matthew 6:22

The verse

"If thine eye be single"

Matthew 6:22

This is one of my favorite lines in the Gospels, and most modern Bibles have translated the mystery right out of it. In the King James it reads like a teaching straight out of the Upanishads.

The verse

Single, healthy, or good?

The light of the body is the eye: if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light.

Matthew 6:22 (KJV)

The Greek word is haplous: single, simple, whole, undivided. Many modern translations say "if your eye is healthy" or "good." They have a reason. The very next verse warns about an "evil eye," and in Jesus' world a good eye meant generosity and an evil eye meant stinginess. It also sits right between "store up treasures in heaven" and "you can't serve God and money." So on one level it's about greed. (More here.)

But a reader of this verse once told me he doesn't trust any translation that swaps "single" for "good," and I get it. The mystics have always heard something more in it.

The mystic reading

Not two

A single eye is an undivided eye, seeing without splitting the world into me and not-me, mine and yours, good and evil. That's nonduality, "not two," the heart of Advaita Vedanta, Zen, and the Christian mystics. When the seeing is whole, the whole body is full of light.

Yogis connect it to the "third eye," the spiritual eye between the eyebrows that turns attention inward. Meister Eckhart, the 14th century German mystic, put it this way:

The eye with which I see God is the same eye with which God sees me.

Meister Eckhart

One eye. One seeing. Nothing separate. Sit with that one for a while.
