Verses / The Good Samaritan

The verses

The Good Samaritan

Luke 10:25–37

"Good Samaritan" has become a cliché, a name for hospitals and nonprofits. But to the people who first heard this story, it was a shock. The hero is the person they'd been taught their whole lives to avoid, and the bad guys are the religious professionals.

The setup

"Who is my neighbor?"

An expert in religious law asks Jesus what he has to do to inherit eternal life. Jesus asks him what the law says. The man answers well: love God with all your heart, soul, strength, and mind, and love your neighbor as yourself. "You have answered correctly," Jesus says. "Do this, and you will live." But the man wants a definition. "Who is my neighbor?"

So Jesus tells a story. A man going down from Jerusalem to Jericho is robbed, stripped, beaten, and left half dead.

The story

Two holy men walk past

By chance a certain priest was going down that way. When he saw him, he passed by on the other side. In the same way a Levite also, when he came to the place, and saw him, passed by on the other side.

Luke 10:31–32

They don't just fail to help. They cross the road. And they probably thought they were following the rules: the law said anyone who touched a dead body would be unclean for seven days (Numbers 19:11). Better not risk it.

But a certain Samaritan, as he traveled, came where he was. When he saw him, he was moved with compassion, came to him, and bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine.

Luke 10:33–34

He puts the man on his own animal, takes him to an inn, pays for his care, and promises to cover whatever else it costs.

Why it stung

Who the Samaritans were

Jews and Samaritans had centuries of bad blood: disputes over the right temple, the right scripture, the right bloodline. Each saw the other as heretics. In John's gospel, when people want to insult Jesus, they say, "Don't we say well that you are a Samaritan, and have a demon?" (John 8:48).

At the end, Jesus asks the lawyer which of the three was a neighbor to the man. The lawyer can't even bring himself to say the word Samaritan. He answers, "He who showed mercy on him." And Jesus says, "Go and do likewise."

Tell this story today and the Samaritan is whoever your community taught you to fear: the immigrant, the Muslim, the atheist, the trans kid, the guy with the other bumper sticker. And the people crossing the road are the ones with the Bible verses. That's the teaching. Love isn't a membership card. It's what you do on the road.
