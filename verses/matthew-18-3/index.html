My Zen master

What my son taught me

When my son was a baby, I'd walk over to him from my desk, carrying all of today's work and tomorrow's to-dos, and the whole pile would get blown out like a candle in a tornado. Suddenly I was dragged into this moment, free of obligations, free of ideology. Through the tears and diapers and sleeplessness of a global pandemic, watching a miniature human crack a grin was pure bliss.

Kids aren't sinless angels. They're grabby and loud. But they're here. They look at a leaf like it's the first leaf. They don't carry yesterday around. I was teaching him concepts. He was teaching me concept-less-ness.

Same teaching, everywhere

Beginner's mind

  • In the Gospel of Thomas, Jesus sees infants nursing and says these little ones are like those who enter the kingdom (saying 22).
  • Zen calls it beginner's mind: seeing everything fresh, without the expert's crust of assumptions.
  • The Tao Te Ching keeps returning to the newborn, soft and supple and whole.

Notice the word "turn." It's the same move as repentance, a change of mind. (More here.) Not becoming childish. Becoming childlike: humble, present, open, and astonished.
