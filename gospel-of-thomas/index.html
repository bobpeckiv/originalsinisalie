A guide

The Gospel of Thomas

114 sayings, found in 1945

In December 1945, near the town of Nag Hammadi in Egypt, farmers dug up a sealed jar full of ancient books. Inside was a collection of 114 sayings of Jesus that most people hadn't read in about 1,600 years. It changes everything.

What it is

A sayings gospel

These are the secret sayings that the living Jesus spoke and Didymos Judas Thomas recorded.

Gospel of Thomas, prologue
  • No plot. No birth story, no miracles, no crucifixion, no resurrection. Just sayings, one after another.
  • The full text survives in Coptic, an Egyptian language. Fragments of the older Greek version had already turned up at Oxyrhynchus, Egypt, around 1900, before anyone knew what they belonged to.
  • About half the sayings have parallels in Matthew, Mark, and Luke, like the mustard seed (20) and the Great Banquet (64).
  • The date is hotly debated. Some scholars put it in the second century. Others argue its core goes back as early as the gospels in the Bible.

Think about it: a simple list of sayings is exactly what you'd expect to come before a polished story with a plot. And when Thomas and the Bible tell the same parable, Thomas is often the simpler version. (See the Great Banquet on the Hell page.)

Sayings that stop me

A few to sit with

  1. Saying 3

    "The kingdom is inside of you, and it is outside of you. When you know yourselves, then you will be known." Luke 17:21, taken all the way. (More here.)

  2. Saying 22

    "When you make the two into one, and when you make the inner like the outer and the outer like the inner... then you will enter the kingdom." Nonduality, in the words of Jesus.

  3. Saying 70

    "If you bring forth what is within you, what you bring forth will save you. If you do not bring forth what is within you, what you do not bring forth will destroy you."

  4. Saying 77

    "Split a piece of wood; I am there. Lift up the stone, and you will find me there." The I AM isn't a man in a robe. It's everywhere.

  5. Saying 113

    "The Father's kingdom is spread out upon the earth, and people don't see it."

Why it got buried

The Gnostics and the canon

Thomas is often called Gnostic, from gnosis, knowing by direct experience. Some scholars call it proto-Gnostic because it seems so early. As the church organized around bishops and creeds in the second and third centuries, books like this were set aside, and many were destroyed. Someone in Egypt cared enough to hide this one in a jar.

You don't need it to be in the Bible for it to be worth reading. Read it next to the Gospels and watch how many sayings you recognize, and how the ones you don't recognize all point inward.
