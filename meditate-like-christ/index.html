Guides / Meditate like Christ

A guide

"Meditate like Christ"

Neem Karoli Baba on Jesus

Imagine flying halfway around the world, away from everything Western, up into the foothills of the Himalayas to sit with a Hindu guru who worships the monkey god. You ask him how to meditate. And he tells you to meditate like Christ.

Who he was

Maharajji

Neem Karoli Baba, known to his devotees as Maharajji, was a Hindu saint who died in 1973. He wrote no books, gave no lectures, and didn't teach from scripture. He sat on a wooden bench wrapped in a plaid blanket, laughed, fed people, and loved everyone who came, from government ministers to beggars. He taught through moments.

A small group of young Americans found him in the late 1960s, and through them he changed the West. Richard Alpert, a psychology professor fired from Harvard, became Ram Dass and wrote Be Here Now. Larry Brilliant went on to help the World Health Organization eradicate smallpox. Daniel Goleman wrote Emotional Intelligence. Krishna Das brought kirtan to American yoga studios. Steve Jobs went to India in 1974 hoping to meet him and arrived months too late.

Larry Brilliant put it best. Of course Maharajji loved everybody, that's what saints do. What amazed him was that when he was sitting with Maharajji, he loved everybody.

The teaching

He lost himself in love

One day his Western devotees asked him how to meditate. He told them to meditate like Christ. They were stunned. They'd come all this way to get away from the religion they grew up with. So they asked: how did Christ meditate? He said:

He lost himself in love. He was one with all beings and he had great love for all in the world... He was one with God... He never died. He is atman living in the hearts of all. See all beings as the reflection of Christ.

Neem Karoli Baba, as recorded in Miracle of Love

That's the Jesus of this whole site. Not a gatekeeper. A man so lost in love that he became one with God, and who lives on as the Self in every heart.

How he taught

Spleen

Ram Dass arrived in India burned out. He'd gone deep with psychedelics, lost his career, and was looking for anyone who actually knew what he'd glimpsed. A young American seeker named Bhagavan Das led him to his guru. The night before they arrived, alone under the stars, Ram Dass thought about his mother, who had died a few months earlier.

The first thing Maharajji did was ask for their Land Rover. The second was to lean in and say, "You were out under the stars last night. You were thinking about your mother." Then, in English: "Spleen. She died of spleen." No one in India knew that. Ram Dass said his rational mind simply shut down, like a computer overloaded with data. He cried, and something in him came home. He stayed, and spent the rest of his life sharing what he found.

What he asked

Feed people

  • Asked how to become enlightened, he said: "Feed people."
  • "Love everyone, serve everyone, remember God."
  • When Ram Dass was beside himself about the refugees in Bangladesh and wanted to rush to help, Maharajji asked him, "Don't you see it's all perfect?" He wasn't telling him not to care. He wept, fed the hungry, and comforted the grieving himself. He was showing him how to serve without being swallowed by the suffering.

A Hindu saint, a devotee of the monkey god, telling Americans to meditate like Jesus and to feed people. If you're looking for proof that the mystics speak one language, it's hard to do better. (More on the mystics.)
