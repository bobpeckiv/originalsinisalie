Jesus, Krishna, Buddha

Three teachers, one message

Put the words of Jesus, Krishna, and the Buddha next to each other and something wonderful happens. Different centuries, different languages, different cultures, and they keep pointing at the same three things: the path is inside you, love everyone, and let go of your grip on the world. Mystics call them avatars, masks of the One.

Same story, same fights

The parallels keep going

  • The temptation. After his baptism, Jesus goes into the desert and refuses Satan's offers of power and glory (Matthew 4). Under the Bodhi tree, the Buddha refuses Mara's offers of pleasure and power, then wakes up.
  • Against empty ritual. Jesus: "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath" (Mark 2:27), and it's not what goes into your mouth that defiles you, but what comes out (Mark 7:15). The Buddha, in the Amagandha Sutta: anger, deceit, envy, and cruelty are what defile a person, not the food they eat.
  • Reformers of their own religion. Jesus was born a Jew, and the Buddha was raised in the Vedic tradition that became Hinduism. Both called their traditions back to the heart. Krishna explains why avatars keep showing up: "Whenever there is decay of righteousness... then I myself come forth" (Bhagavad Gita 4.7).

Swami Vivekananda, who loved Jesus, read that verse this way: whenever you see a great soul struggling to uplift humanity, know that it's the One, come again. Jesus is wonderful. He got it. My only question is why we'd stop at him.
